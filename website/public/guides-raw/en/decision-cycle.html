One decision cycle · theory

ADI · DDR · Trust Calculus

Three concepts that turn "we discussed that back then" into a reproducible process. ADI teaches you to write down at least three versions before a fix, DDR records the decision and the conditions for revisiting it, and Trust Calculus rates the strength of each piece of evidence. Together they add up to one cycle - and this walkthrough shows it.

01 · The ADI cycle - how an investigator works

Concept 01 · the investigator analogy

A good investigator does not lock onto the first version

Picture an investigator at a crime scene. A poor investigator locks onto the first version ("it is the neighbour, they had a motive") and the rest of the investigation looks for confirmations. A good investigator works differently: first they put forward three or four versions, for each one they formulate "if this is true, such-and-such traces should be there", and only then do they go and check. If the traces do not match, the version drops out. What remains is the one that survived every check.

The ADI cycle is exactly the same way of thinking, only about architectural decisions.

  • Abduction · generating hypotheses. At least three. Not one, not two - three. The threshold is exactly that, because two hypotheses almost always collapse into a false dichotomy ("either Redis or Postgres"), and a third hypothesis often breaks that dichotomy and turns out to be the most interesting one ("an in-process LRU with no network at all"). Three is the working rule of this course against anchoring on the first version, not a statistical threshold.
  • Deduction · logical inference. For each hypothesis we formulate "if it is true, then this should be observed". If not a single consequence can be checked, it is not a hypothesis, it is a nice-sounding sentence.
  • Induction · checking against facts. A benchmark, a test, a link to documentation, a talk with the team, a measurement. Every piece of evidence confirms or refutes one specific prediction from the deduction. Not "here is an article" but "here is an article, and it refutes hypothesis 2".
Worked example · Vault hybrid search

In the Knowledge Vault, hybrid search currently runs on sqlite-vec. Suppose that in three months a requirement shows up: "search 50k notes with p99 < 100 ms". Without ADI you or the agent start fixing the first thing that comes to mind - "let us add Qdrant". Half an hour of work, and it turns out the bottleneck was not the cache but the way reranking is done.

With ADI you first write down three hypotheses: (1) sqlite-vec cannot handle 50k, (2) reranking on the Node side takes 80% of the time, (3) RRF with k=60 gives too wide a window. For each one you formulate a checkable consequence. Then you go and look at EXPLAIN ANALYZE and the traces. Five minutes - hypothesis 2 is confirmed. The agent does not fly off for 30 minutes fixing the wrong thing.

The key difference

ADI does not slow you down. You spend 10 minutes on diagnosis instead of 30 minutes on random fixes. This is not about slowing down - it is about not running in the wrong direction.

02 · DDR - a court ruling for architectural choices

Concept 02 · the court analogy

The rationale section of a technical decision

So you have run ADI and picked a decision. The next question is: what do you do with it, so that in three months a colleague (or you yourself) can understand why it was done exactly this way?

The analogy is a court ruling. A judge does not just say "guilty" or "not guilty". The judge writes the rationale: which evidence was examined, which was rejected and why, on what basis the conclusion was reached. A year later, if new circumstances appear, the case can be reopened - and the next judge has the full picture instead of "that is how it was decided back then".

DDR (Detailed Decision Record) is the rationale for your technical decision. A markdown file in the repo - this course uses docs/decisions/DDR-NNN-<slug>.md - with six mandatory sections. That is a convention of this course, not a separate artifact kind in Forgeplan: there the same fields live inside an ADR, where the mandatory sections are ## Context, ## Decision and ## Consequences:

DDR-042 · Lowering candidateLimit in search active
01 · Context
The situation, the constraints, the requirements. One or two paragraphs.
02 · Options considered
At least three - that is the exit from Abduction.
03 · Predictions and evidence
"If X, then Y; here is the measurement / link". That is Deduction + Induction together.
04 · Accepted decision
One sentence.
05 · Rejected alternatives
Each with a concrete reason for rejection. Not "it did not fit" but "it did not fit, because it needs a separate DevOps resource and we have one".
06 · Conditions for revisiting
On which event, metric or date this decision should be opened again. For example: "revisit if the notes pass 100k, or if p99 drops below 50 ms, or after 6 months".
⚡ Evidence Decay
The sixth item is the most important part

Without conditions for revisiting, a DDR turns into a monument: the decision was taken, written into the code and forgotten. A year later RPS has grown tenfold, but "we do have a DDR about the cache" - and nobody opens it. With conditions for revisiting a DDR stays alive - it has a shelf life.

That is what Evidence Decay means: evidence applicability can change. A 2023 benchmark for Redis 6.x does not necessarily apply to Redis 7.4; numbers for Vault at 50 notes need checking before transfer to 50 000. A good DDR records those limits.

On length

A DDR should be short - 200-400 words, one page. If it comes to 2000 words, you will already not come back to this document in a month, and the point is lost. It is not an ADR, not an RFC, not a technical document for review. It is a note to your future self.

03 · Trust Calculus - an inspection of evidence quality

Concept 03 · the inspector analogy

Every claim is rated along three independent axes

Now you have a DDR. How do you know the evidence in it is strong enough to base a decision on?

The analogy is a construction inspection. An inspector does not mark "good" or "bad". They assess along concrete axes: conformance to the specification, quality of materials, assembly tolerances. Each axis is independent, each has its own score, and the overall assessment is not an average but the ability to tell an object you can hand over from one you cannot put a roof on.

Trust Calculus is the same thing for your claims. Every claim a decision relies on is rated along three independent axes, each on a 0-9 scale. The 0-9 scale is a teaching layout for this lesson: it shows the "weakest link" principle, but it is not what Forgeplan computes - see the block at the end of this section.

  • F · Formality - how strictly it is worded. 0: "it feels like it". 5: an explicit claim with conditions ("under load X the metric is Y"). 9: a formal specification or proof.
  • G · Granularity - how concrete it is. 0: "it is slow". 5: "15% faster". 9: "p99 = 47 ms at 10k RPS, 1 KB payload, setup described".
  • R · Reliability - where the source comes from. 0: a story from Slack. 5: several independent sources. 9: a peer-reviewed paper or your own production benchmark.

The axes are independent. A beautiful marketing whitepaper from a vendor may be F7 G8 R2 - formal, detailed, but the source is selling you its product. A Slack screenshot from a colleague with a reproducible benchmark may be F2 G8 R7 - badly presented, but the numbers are real and reproducible.

The lesson scale and the Forgeplan computation are different things

The 0–9 axes and "WLNK" above are a teaching model, not a universal FPF formula. Forgeplan separately computes an artifact's F/G/R on a 0–1 scale and their geometric mean (F × G × R)^(1/3). These are artifact-quality heuristics. An evidence pack's intrinsic score does not use the teaching F/G/R ratings. In the full scorer, an unexpired pack scores max(0, verdict − CL penalty − type modifier). Bases for supports/weakens/refutes are 1.0 / 0.5 / 0.0; CL0 / CL1 / CL2 / CL3 penalties are 0.9 / 0.4 / 0.1 / 0; modifiers for measurement/test/benchmark/audit are 0 / 0 / 0.1 / 0.2. If the optional valid_until has expired, the scorer returns 0.1 before this arithmetic, including for refutes. With no date, this scorer applies no expiry. Recursive decision R_eff takes the minimum of its own eligible evidence score and eligible dependency contributions. Evidence packs in draft count; superseded/deprecated packs are excluded. Dependencies also exclude draft and the note/memory kinds. A dependency contributes max(0, dependency R_eff − relation penalty): based_on/informs incur 0.1 (CL2), refines/depends_on incur 0 (CL3). Own evidence at 1.0 and an eligible based_on dependency at 0.2 produce 0.1 overall. With no eligible own evidence, an ordinary decision scores 0; a leaf evidence pack is scored on its own fields. The flat helper used for some displayed scores neither traverses dependencies nor applies the type modifier. Its result can differ from the full recursive report.

Rule of thumb for a DDR

If a decision rests on claims whose F+G+R sum is below 12, it is a weak decision, and before you commit it is worth collecting more evidence. You do not have to push everything to 9/9/9 - that is expensive and usually unnecessary. The goal is not perfection but honesty with yourself. If you see 2/2/2 ("a colleague said so") you know that you are taking a risk, and that is a deliberate decision rather than self-deception. The threshold of 12 is a sum, not a weakest link: three average scores add up to 12 while one of the axes is very weak. It is a convention of this lesson (the 3D deep dive uses it too), not a Forgeplan threshold.

The cycle on one example · Vault hybrid search

How all three concepts add up in one pass - from the first version to a written DDR with conditions for revisiting.

01 · ADI

Three hypotheses, before touching anything

H1: sqlite-vec cannot cope with 50k. H2: reranking on Node takes 80% of the time. H3: RRF with k=60 gives too wide a window. For each one - a checkable consequence.

02 · Evidence

Collecting facts and rating them by F/G/R

EXPLAIN ANALYZE refutes H1 (the index answers in 18-24 ms). A trace on the test bench confirms H2: 71% of the time in rerank. An internal benchmark of lowering k shows −22% latency - that is F7 G9 R7, WLNK = 7.

03 · DDR

The rationale section · 6 sections, 280 words

Decision: rewrite the reranking as a Rust extension. The alternatives (Qdrant, k=20) are rejected with concrete reasons. Conditions for revisiting: reopen if the notes pass 100k, or if p99 rises above 80 ms, or after 6 months.

04 · Decay

After 6 months — a reason to start a review

After the specified valid_until date has passed, the record can appear in an overdue-artifact report. That is a derived date-check result, not an automatic DDR reopening, status change or evidence recheck. A person or explicitly configured external automation initiates the review. The numbers may turn out to be stale (a new sqlite-vec release, the database growing to 200k); the team then runs ADI again with three hypotheses under this lesson's rule. The team or a configured external monitor also checks metrics and events — there are no built-in triggers for them. If forgeplan reopen is used, the source artifact must be active or stale: the command creates a new draft with a Lineage section and a based_on edge to the source, then marks the source deprecated.
