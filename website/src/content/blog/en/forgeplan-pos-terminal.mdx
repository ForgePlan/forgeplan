> **Scope, clarified 23 October 2026.** This dated essay uses analogies and a worked Vault scenario, not a measured terminal session. Timings, coverage percentages, latency values, IDs and successful outcomes are illustrative unless separately evidenced. ForgePlan adapts BMAD/OpenSpec/FPF ideas; its templates and scoring are not literal universal upstream rules. The four depth labels are the series' teaching model; current core treats Critical as a Deep alias. Nothing here waives project-specific validation, review or release gates.

## The POS analogy

When a waiter takes an order, they don't hold the price list, the dish ingredients, the VAT rate, discount cards, and loyalty points in their head. They press buttons on a POS terminal, and the terminal remembers everything.

The waiter's job is to choose a dish, clarify the customer's preferences, carry over the drink. The terminal's job is to format it correctly, calculate the total, apply the right discount, send the order to the kitchen, print the receipt. These two roles are separated - and that's exactly why a restaurant scales: a new waiter is trained in an hour, because they don't need to memorize the price list.

In software development we traditionally require engineers to hold the price list, the dishes, and the tax rate in their heads simultaneously. What format does a PRD use? How many sections? Where does the decision record go? How do you link it to tests? When do you reassess an architectural choice? Every team reinvents its own set of habits, and every new developer spends their first weeks "learning our decision culture."

I want to break down what a "POS terminal for development methodologies" looks like - and why its role isn't "yet another methodology on top," but rather a tool that holds already-existing methods in one plane and takes the mechanical work off the human.

## What Forgeplan takes off your plate

In previous posts in this series I walked through several ways of working: BMAD for designing features through four phases; OpenSpec for disciplined specification work through the delta format and dependency graph; the ADI principle for generating three versions before committing; the R_eff formula for rating the reliability of decisions through the weakest evidence link.

Each of these methods was developed independently, with its own authors and communities. But in real work they complement each other: BMAD designs the feature, ADI picks one of three architectural options, OpenSpec fixes the contract between components, R_eff rates the reliability of the whole package.

Forgeplan is a tool that assembles these four approaches into a single workflow. Concretely:

- `forgeplan new prd` creates ForgePlan's local template, with thirteen substantive sections plus Progress; required checks depend on depth. It is not a universal thirteen-section BMAD standard.
- `forgeplan new spec` creates a structural API/data-model template; behavioral Requirements/Scenario specifications are also supported. ADDED / MODIFIED / REMOVED is a useful delta convention, not the default generated template.
- `forgeplan new adr` creates the actual ADR template, with context, decision, consequences and depth-dependent sections including validity/review conditions. It is not the six-field DDR writing convention used in this series.
- Evidence scoring reads body fields such as `verdict`, `congruence_level` and `evidence_type`. Product `fgr` separately computes Formality, Granularity and Reliability on 0–1 scales with a geometric-mean overall score; it does not read three manual 0–9 evidence axes.

These formats weren't invented from scratch - they come from public sources: BMAD, OpenSpec, ideas on three-axis evidence rating from decision-making literature. Forgeplan just assembles them into one workflow and doesn't let you skip the mandatory parts.

## What stays with the human

A POS terminal doesn't choose for the waiter what to prepare - it only formats the choice. Forgeplan doesn't write the content of decisions for the team - it only provides the right format for recording them.

What stays with the human:

- **The content of the decision.** Which architectural option to pick. Which alternatives to consider. What reasons to reject some and accept others.
- **Business context.** Which trade-offs are acceptable, which aren't. Which client matters. Where the boundaries of scope lie.
- **Understanding the team.** Who can work with which technology. Where you're strong, where you're weak.
- **The final call.** Among the three versions the AI generated and the five the tech lead proposed - which one is right for this specific context.

The human retains the substantive work. ForgePlan provides templates and checks that can expose omissions; neither a template nor a score prevents all missing context or proves that the implementation meets the decision.

## Ten record types, six in active use

This article focuses on the ten kinds exposed by the ordinary `new` interface. The core taxonomy also has brownfield kinds; six commonly useful kinds are listed first. No measured 95% task-coverage rate is established here.

The active six:

- **PRD** (Product Requirements Document) - a product requirement with 13 sections. What we're building and why.
- **RFC** (Request for Comments) - an architectural proposal with implementation phases. How we're building it.
- **ADR** (Architecture Decision Record) - a recorded choice with context, alternatives, consequences and depth-dependent checks.
- **Spec** - a system behavior or API/data contract; changes can be documented as deltas.
- **Epic** - a group of PRDs/RFCs/ADRs unified by a common goal. For when a task is too large for one PRD.
- **Evidence** - recorded support or counter-evidence with verdict, context and provenance. What we're basing the decision on.

The four situational types:

- **Problem** - a record of a problem with context, before choosing a solution.
- **Solution** - an examination of two or three options for solving the problem.
- **Note** - a micro-decision with an optional, explicitly assigned `valid_until` date; creation does not supply a default 90-day TTL. Expiry can be reported; it does not itself delete the record or change its persisted status.
- **Refresh** - a re-evaluation of a stale decision.

Each record is a Markdown file in `.forgeplan/`. Canonical typed relations use a frontmatter `links` array with `target` and `relation` entries, not arbitrary `parent`, `informs` or `supersedes` scalar keys. Files share the code repository's branches and review history.

## Routing: which set of records do you need?

`forgeplan route "your task"` suggests depth and an artifact pipeline. Latency depends on routing mode and any configured LLM; a half-second response is not a contract.

The routing logic is based on four depth levels:

- **Tactical**: a trivial task, reversible within a day. One file, one developer. No records, no ADR. Just code and a commit message.
- **Standard**: a feature taking 1-3 days with one meaningful choice. PRD + RFC.
- **Deep**: an irreversible task taking 1-2 weeks with an architectural decision. PRD + Spec + RFC + ADR. ADI (three-version analysis) - required.
- **Critical**: a cross-team task, a strategy. Epic + multiple PRDs + multiple Specs + multiple RFCs + multiple ADRs. ADI + review.

The routing recommendation is advisory. Teams should inspect the reasons and decide against their actual risk and reversibility. The article's former 80% acceptance and one-hour saving claims have no cited measurement and are not product guarantees.

## Checks before approval

`forgeplan validate` checks the document against ForgePlan's implemented, depth-dependent rules: required sections, content heuristics, contracts and other supported findings. It is not a universal semantic proof of metrics, alternatives, all cross-spec versions or implementation behavior.

Format, scope, alternative quality and consistency remain useful review questions. Diagnostic examples such as "edge cases empty" or "reference outdated" are illustrative, not captured output or a promise that each is automatically detected. Run independent tests and review to establish behavior.

Teams that work with checklists for code builds - linters, formatters - quickly understand the analogy. `forgeplan validate` is a linter for decisions.

## Activation records approval, not file immutability

`forgeplan activate` changes status after the applicable checks. Active bodies and titles remain editable through `update`; the file is not technically immutable. For a materially replaced decision, create its successor and use `forgeplan supersede OLD --by NEW` to preserve explicit lineage. Git and review practices preserve edit history.

This gives you an honest history of decisions: you can see what was decided then, what was decided later, and in what order. Not "a file overwritten without a trace," but a timeline. Two years from now you can open ADR-005, see "superseded by ADR-019 in April 2027 for reason X," and go to ADR-019 for the current version. ADR-005 is open for reading, but marked as historical.

Without this mechanism, teams lose historical context. Someone overwrites the old ADR with a new one, and a year later it's impossible to answer "why did we first pick JWT and then switch to sessions." The file shows only the final state. With the supersede mechanism - both ADRs are accessible, the reasons for transitions are visible, and any decision can be traced through time.

## A concrete example: the streaming endpoint for Vault

In this illustrative scenario, Vault adds `GET /query/stream`. The commands name a possible workflow, not exact returned IDs, timings or responses. Use the actual IDs from `new`; inspect `route` rather than assuming it must choose Deep.

`forgeplan route "Add streaming endpoint GET /query/stream"` - inspect the recommended depth and pipeline.

`forgeplan new prd "Streaming endpoint for model queries"` - creates PRD-018 with 13 sections, ready to fill. The project manager takes 30 minutes to fill in the goal, audience, metrics (first token in 800ms, total response time in 5 seconds), functional requirements, non-goals (not caching streaming in MVP), risks (a slow model provider delays the first token).

`forgeplan validate PRD-018` - inspect all real findings and fill the required sections. The earlier three-check/empty-edge-case output was an illustration.

`forgeplan reason PRD-018` - requires a configured LLM provider and asks for competing approaches. For this scenario, compare SSE, WebSockets and chunked HTTP streaming; HTTP/2 server push is not an interchangeable application-response streaming transport.

`forgeplan new rfc "Streaming via SSE"` - creates an RFC template. Explicitly link it to the actual PRD with `forgeplan link RFC-007 PRD-018 --relation based_on`; `new` does not infer `parent: PRD-018`.

`forgeplan new adr "Use SSE for streaming responses"` - fill the real ADR template, alternatives and review conditions, then link its actual dependencies.

`forgeplan new evidence "SSE benchmark on staging"` - creates a template, not a benchmark. A p50 first-token of 180ms is an illustrative result here, not a measured observation. Real evidence must record method, environment and results; use scoring body fields such as `verdict: supports`, `congruence_level: 3`, `evidence_type: measurement`, not F=7/G=8/R=8.

`forgeplan link EVID-001 ADR-012 --relation informs` - links the evidence to the decision.

`forgeplan score ADR-012` - inspect the real result. The former R_eff=0.9 was illustrative and did not follow from the manual F/G/R tuple; scoring and validation are distinct checks.

`forgeplan activate ADR-012` - changes status only if the applicable gates permit it. Ordinary gates check length, linked evidence, stubs and MUST findings, not an R_eff threshold. Approval does not freeze the file.

Six months later, a human review or separately configured monitor might detect tenfold traffic or p95 approaching 700ms. `forgeplan stale` detects expired `valid_until` dates, not business telemetry or prose triggers. Renewal requires a legal stored-status transition plus `--reason` and `--until`; otherwise choose the appropriate explicit revision/reopen/supersede workflow.
